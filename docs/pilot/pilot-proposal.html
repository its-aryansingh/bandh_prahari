Baandh Prahari: a one-district pilotबाँध प्रहरी · integration map, pilot plan, costs, data protection and roadmap

For the Water Resources Department (WRD), the Disaster Management Department (DMD) and BSDMA · October 2026 · Draft for discussion

This note goes with the two-page concept note. It sets out what Baandh Prahari reads from and hands back to the state's existing systems, what a pilot in one district for one monsoon would need and measure, what it would cost, how it protects people's data, and how it could grow. Anything we could not confirm from a primary source is marked to confirm, to be settled with the department.

1. How it works, in one incident

ReportA patrol worker sends a voice note and photo on WhatsApp, or the helpline logs a call.
→
RulesPublished rules set P1, P2 or P3 from the sign and the river. No model decides.
→
WarnFor a P1, the mukhiya, ward members and subscribed families get "get ready" at once.
→
PageThe junior engineer on duty gets the kilometre, photo, trend and nearest team.
→
ClimbNo answer in 10 minutes (or no delivery in 2): AE, then EE and district, then the zone's force.
→
RecordEvery step written as it happens, locked, and handed to the district.

Relief runs on the same clock: a family's call for help goes to the nearest free relief team; a team that says "on my way" has 20 minutes before the next team is asked and the district is told.

3. The pilot: Saran, monsoon 2027

Saran is where the Haijalpur breach happened this season, and where the prototype's map, gauge (Rewaghat) and villages already are. One division, one monsoon, with nothing installed in the field.

Who does what

  • WRD nodal officer: the roster, the stretches by chainage, the escalation chain, weekly review.
  • District (DM office, control room): receives climbs, approves any public alert, uses the briefing.
  • DMD / BSDMA: relief teams on the map; the relief-list format.
  • Patrol workers and engineers: save one number; a 30-minute briefing.
  • Our team: deploys into the department's AWS account, runs drills, reports weekly, hands over the code and the account.

Timeline

Dec–FebMoU and data access; WhatsApp number in the department's name; AWS account
Mar–MayLoad roster and chainage; train patrol workers; two dry-run drills with the district

4. What it costs

An estimate for one district's monsoon from AWS's published prices, assuming 150 km of embankment, 150 patrol workers, 20 high-water nights with five roll-calls each, 500 reports and 150 incidents.

ItemPer season
WhatsApp messages through AWS End User Messaging Social (AWS's fee: up to $0.005 per message out, $0.001 in)~ $115
Amazon Transcribe, Amazon Bedrock and Amazon Polly~ $5
Step Functions, Lambda, DynamoDB, S3, Location Service, EventBridge Scheduler~ $10
CloudWatch and Amplify Hosting~ $25
Total~ $155, plus Meta's fee on template messages

Most of the cost is roll-calls. The services are pay-per-use, so the cost from November to May is close to nothing. People's time (briefings, drills) is the larger cost and is the department's to plan. For scale, the 2024 Gandak flood damaged 118 km of embankments and cost ₹512 crore.7

5. Data protection and security

Personal data (DPDP Act 2023)

  • The Act allows processing to assist any individual during a disaster (section 7(h)).8 We still tell every reporter what their number is used for.
  • Help requests carry no names; the family's number is stored only as a hash for de-duplication.
  • Subscriptions to village warnings are kept with a consent note, for that season only.
  • The public map shows counts per village; exact locations only to relief teams and the district, with a role login (a pilot adds MFA).
  • Team positions only from team phones on duty. Nobody else is tracked.

Security and ownership

  • Runs in AWS Mumbai (ap-south-1), a MeitY-empanelled region; data stays in India.9
  • Logs kept 180 days, as CERT-In's 2022 directions ask.10
  • Encryption with AWS KMS; a write-once record (S3 Object Lock); one least-privilege role per function.
  • Open source (Apache-2.0), deployed by one template into the department's own AWS account. The state owns the code, the account and the data.
  • No public alert is ever sent by the system: drafts are marked Exercise until the authorised officer sends them.

6. Risks, and what we would do

RiskWhat we would do
It is read as blaming engineersThe same record proves a fast answer; the page hands the engineer the photo, trend and nearest team at once. No individual is named in public reports.
Phones are off or out of coverageA page with no delivery receipt moves on in two minutes; a later phase adds government SMS and calls.
False alarms erode trust"Get ready" asks only for cheap steps; "move" needs an engineer's confirmation or two reports from the same spot; the mukhiya can cancel a false alarm, and every cancel is logged.
A government feed is downThe last good reading is kept, and the screen says how old it is; GloFAS is the fallback for the river curve.
Voice notes in Bhojpuri or Maithili are misreadThe worker's tap on the sign list sets the report; the voice note travels with it untouched.

A note on the reference blueprints. They were drafted with an AI tool and are treated as leads, not facts. Before any of their components is proposed, each number and system name will be checked with the department, and each AWS service checked for availability to new customers in ap-south-1. For example, AWS has closed Amazon Timestream for LiveAnalytics to new customers,11 so a time-series store would use another service. One blueprint places the Haijalpur breach near km 46.5 of the Saran embankment; our map uses its own kilometre marks (km 14), and the official chainage is to confirm. Forecasting would build on CWC's and the department's official forecasts rather than a new model.
